# The Starred Bill: notes for Claude

Compares dinner, lunch and wine pairing prices at Michelin-starred restaurants, with a page per country, region and city. Live at https://starredbill.com (GitHub repo sbarnard310/starred-bill). Each country's pages offer English plus its main official language (Chinese and Cantonese for Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau, Chinese also for Singapore at the owner's request; Simplified Chinese for mainland China; French, Japanese, Spanish, Italian, Korean, Danish, Swedish, Icelandic, Catalan, Thai, German, Dutch, Portuguese, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Slovenian, Croatian, Serbian, Greek, Turkish, Lithuanian, Latvian, Estonian, Maltese, Malay, Filipino, Vietnamese, Arabic for Qatar and the UAE; Switzerland has German, French and Italian, Belgium Dutch and French, Luxembourg French and German, all at the owner's choice on 5 Oct 2026); the homepage, account and privacy pages are English only. README.md documents every data field. Keep it in step when fields change.

## How it's built
- `content/` is the data, edited by the owner in Pages CMS (app.pagescms.org) or on GitHub. There is one JSON file per restaurant in `content/restaurants/<country>/`, one per place in `content/places/`, plus `currencies.json` and `site.json`.
- `content/world-starred.json` lists every Michelin-starred restaurant in the world: name, stars, position and Michelin link. It drives the outlined "no prices yet" pins on the homepage map. It also gives the homepage's "Pick a country" cards for countries without a page yet (star counts and a button that frames them on the map); `WORLD_COUNTRIES` in build.py maps the guide's country names to our ids and English/Chinese names, and the build lists any new country it doesn't know. Below the cards, `content/no-stars.json` lists the UN member countries with no starred restaurant, by region, with an optional `note`/`noteZh` on why (most simply have no MICHELIN Guide; checked 4 Oct 2026 from the guide's own search, where only Saudi Arabia has a guide without stars), and an optional `guide` (a guide's id) that links the country's name to it. Starless countries with search demand get an explainer guide rather than a place page, which would add an empty country card: `michelin-star-restaurants-india`, `-australia` and `-south-africa` (5 Oct 2026, plus `michelin-star-indian-restaurants-london`). When one gains stars, add its place pages, rewrite the guide as launch coverage linking to them, and drop it from no-stars.json. The build hides any country that has gained stars and asks for it to be deleted from that file. The cards can be filtered by continent (`CONTINENTS` in build.py; the build lists any country missing from it) and sorted A–Z, by starred restaurants, or by three-, two- or one-star restaurants, high to low or (clicking the chosen button again) low to high; the counts used come from the whole guide (`guide` on each country, from world-starred.json), since our pages may cover only some cities, and cards show that guide total when it's larger. Our own restaurants are matched by name and position (`same_restaurant` in build.py) and shown as filled pins with prices instead. To refresh it after big guide releases, follow `scripts/michelin_world.js` and `scripts/receive.py`. guide.michelin.com blocks curl, so the extraction runs in a browser tab and is posted to a local receiver.
- `src/` holds the HTML templates (`place.html`, `home.html`) and the shared `assets/`:
  - `common.js` has the settings, all English/Chinese wording (`I18N`) and helpers.
  - `place.js` runs destination pages and `home.js` runs the homepage.
  - `site.css` holds all styles.
  - The header: on computers the top row and the breadcrumb bar (`.switchbar`: breadcrumbs, currency, language, Install app) both stay on screen, inside `.masthead`. On phones and tablets (1140px and below, where the full header no longer fits one line) only one row stays: logo, search (jumps to the page's search box, else the homepage's `#search`), wishlist and a menu button (`#menuBtn`) that drops down the section links, Sign in and Share (`.header-menu`; on tablets a panel at the right). The breadcrumb bar scrolls away. On phones (720px and below) the wishlist shows just its heart, and the currency switch moves to a small picker beside Dinner/Lunch (`#currencyPick`). Keep all six page templates' headers the same shape.
- `build.py` uses only the standard library and must stay **Python 3.9 compatible**, because that's the Mac's version. It checks the data, then writes `_site/`: one page per place (places with no stars yet get a short "none yet" page), the homepage, the sitemap and the 404 page. Bad data stops the build with a plain-English list of problems.
- Guides (`content/guides/<id>.json`, rendered by `build_guides()` with `src/guide.html`): explainer articles at `/guides/<id>/`, plus a list at `/guides/`, English only, with Article, FAQPage and breadcrumb JSON-LD. Their figures (`{{n3}}`, `{{price1}}`…) are filled from the restaurant data by `guide_stats()`, and `<a data-guide="id">` links switch on once that guide exists (README lists both). Write them to the owner's content briefs (Google Doc "Michelin Star Site: First-Batch Content Briefs": house style, then one brief per page): answer first in 40–50 words, stars only from the MICHELIN Guide, prices with US dollars, every table dated. Every page's footer links to the "What is a Michelin star?" pillar. Each guide lists its brief's target `keywords` (main one first); the build prints any that don't appear in the page. The list guides (by country, three-star world/UK/London) drop in tables with `{{table:name}}`, built from the restaurant data and matched to MICHELIN Guide links via world-starred.json. The Green Star is being phased out (Michelin, 18 May 2026, replaced by the Mindful Voices editorial platform), so write about it in the past tense. Brief 7's city guides are covered by the destination pages, not separate guides.
- Near me (`/near-me/`, `build_near_me()` in build.py, `src/near.html`, `src/assets/near.js`): finds the visitor by "Use my location" or a typed town or postcode (Google Places `searchByText`), then shows every starred restaurant within a chosen distance on a map and in a list, nearest first, with quick-answer cards (nearest, cheapest, nearest two- and three-star) and the meal, stars, dietary and sort filters. The restaurants come from `/data/near.json` (one compact row each, columns listed in the file). The location never leaves the browser; "Remember this location" keeps it in localStorage `starredbill-near`. English only, with static Popular cities and FAQ (FAQPage JSON-LD) for search engines. Every page's menu links to it ("Near me"), and destination pages' "see what's near you" link opens it with `?locate=1`; `?q=Bath` searches a place straight away.
- Till receipt: destination pages show prices as a cream till receipt (opened phone rows: Dinner, Lunch and Wine pairing lines, a meal + wine total, the country's service line, "Checked Oct 2026" and the source; computers: a torn-off stub in the price column). `receipt()` and `stub()` in place.js, `.receipt`/`.stub` and the `--rc-` colour tokens in site.css (the paper stays cream in dark mode). The homepage's wishlist adds up as one bill (`renderBill()` in home.js) with Dinner/Lunch, an "Add wine pairings" box and £/€/US$, choices kept in localStorage `starredbill-bill`; its lunch and wine prices come from `/data/bill.json` (`build_bill_data()`), loaded only when the wishlist has something in it. Each country's service rule (`SERVICE` in common.js: a kind for the footer line, and the percentage usually added on top for service, plus tax or tip where those come on top) drives both; a new country needs adding there, else it reads "before service" and adds nothing.
- Help me pick (`/pick/`, `build_pick()` in build.py, `src/pick.html`, `src/assets/pick.js`): six questions, one per screen (where: a place, near me or anywhere; lunch, dinner or whichever's cheaper; budget per person in £/€/$ with an optional wine pairing; stars; food mood; dietary needs), each option showing how many restaurants it leaves. Then three picks with a reason each: best match (most stars, then a review-weighted Google rating, wishlist boost), best value (least per star) and a wildcard (newly starred or promoted, else a different cuisine), plus a "stretch your budget" lunch tip, more matches, and one-tap ways out when nothing fits. It reads `/data/near.json` (Near me's file, with `reviews`, `wine`, `lunchWine` and `change` columns for this page). With no pairing price listed, wine is reckoned at 60% of the menu and shown as an estimate. Answers go in the address (`?w=/uk/england/london/&m=dinner&b=200&c=GBP&wine=1&s=2&f=japanese&d=vegan&go=1`), so results can be shared; `?w=` alone answers the first question. Signed-in visitors' "been there" restaurants are left out (with a button to include them). English only; every page's menu links to it ("Help me pick", `navPick` in every dictionary). On destination pages the computer-width header shows Near me and Share as icons only, so it stays on one line.
- Compare (`/compare/`, `build_compare()` in build.py, `src/compare.html`, `src/assets/compare.js`): the visitor ticks two or three restaurants from their wishlist and sees them as till receipts side by side (dinner, lunch, wine pairing and the meal-plus-wine total, lined up across receipts with CSS subgrid), all in one currency picked from every currency in currencies.json (kept in prefs `compareCurrency`; until chosen, the currency the ticked ones share, else a guess from the browser's region). A tick marks the lowest price on each line, and a sentence under the receipts says which is cheapest and by how much. Prices come from `/data/compare/<n>.json`: the starred restaurants shared between 64 small files by `compare_part()` of their id (`partOf()` in compare.js works it out the same way), so the page loads only the files holding the wishlist and any shared link. The ticked ids sit in the address (`?r=id,id`) so a comparison can be shared, and in prefs `compare` for next time. English only, `noindex`, not in the sitemap. Linked from the homepage and account wishlists and each destination page's wishlist note (`wishCompare`) once two or more are saved. The receipts' footer settings (`SERVICE_KIND`, `PRICES_CHECKED`) live in common.js for both pages.
- Page weight (6 Oct 2026, so the biggest pages load quickly on phones): destination pages carry only the translations they offer (`only_langs()` in build.py; restaurant names stay in every script, as they show under each other) and draw their list 40 rows at a time, loading more as it scrolls into view (`LEDGER_STEP` in place.js; the "No longer starred" rows follow the last batch). The homepage's restaurants are in `/data/home.json` (`build_home_data()`, compact rows with a list of places, fetched by an inline script in home.html's head), with its figures and cards drawn first from counts in the page. The account page loads `/data/account.json` only once someone is signed in. Data files built with `write_data()` carry `?v=` versions, so the service worker keeps them until they change.
- `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds and publishes on every push to `main`. Pages `build_type` is `workflow`, and the custom domain is set in the repo's Pages settings. DNS is at Namecheap.
- `.pages.yml` defines the Pages CMS forms. Any new data field needs adding there too (`settings.content.merge: true` keeps unknown keys). The editor saves blank fields as `""`, which `tidy()` in build.py drops.

## Data rules
- A restaurant file's name is its id, and visitors' wishlists store it. Never rename a published restaurant file.
- Prices are numbers in the country's currency, per person, before service. Record where each price came from:
  - `sourceType`: `site` (the restaurant's own website), `press` (a review or booking site) or `none`.
  - `source`: the link.
  - The lunch equivalents are `lunchSourceType` and `lunchSource`.
- When a restaurant loses its stars or closes, keep it: set `stars` to 0 and fill in `status`, `formerStars` and `statusNote`. Ones still open (`lost` or `changed`) that lost their stars in the last three years (by `changeDate`) get small grey pins on the homepage and destination maps (`lostPin` in common.js), so give them `lat`/`lng`.
- A restaurant's `city` is a place id. It shows on that place and on every place above it, plus any `group` that includes them. A region or country can hold restaurants directly: Hong Kong, Macau, Singapore, Ireland, and UK restaurants outside the cities we cover. These sit in their county where it has a page (e.g. `yorkshire`, `cumbria`, `perthshire`), otherwise in their nation (`england`, `scotland`, `wales`, `northern-ireland` or `channel-islands`), with "Town, County" in `area`. Every ceremonial county in England has a page (Yorkshire is one page for all four Yorkshire counties; London and Bristol are cities directly under England). When a city page is added (e.g. York inside Yorkshire), move its restaurants' `city` to it.
- US cities sit inside their state (Illinois, California, Florida, New York State), so a state page can grow when more of its cities are added. Washington DC sits directly under the United States. Los Angeles includes Santa Monica, Beverly Hills, West Hollywood, Culver City, Encino and Long Beach; Miami includes Miami Beach, Coral Gables and North Miami. Each state (or DC) sets its own `starsText` for its guide.
- France has a page for each of its 13 regions (restaurants there show "Town, Département"), with city pages for Lyon, Marseille, Nice, Bordeaux, Toulouse, Montpellier, Strasbourg, Reims, Dijon, Nantes and Courchevel inside their regions; Paris stays directly under France, and the Île-de-France page holds the rest of that region. The research and rebuild scripts are in starred-bill-research/france (fr_build.py; hand-checked prices go in fr_manual.py).
- The 29 countries added on 5 Oct 2026 (Germany to Liechtenstein) came from one pipeline in starred-bill-research/world (w_algolia → w_places → w_crawl → w_extract → w_build; w_state.json lists what it wrote, so a rerun replaces only those files). Countries with 60+ restaurants have a page per MICHELIN region (city-states like Berlin, Hamburg, Vienna, Beijing and Shanghai are their region's page), and towns with 5+ restaurants get a city page. Their official languages were added the same day (see Languages above). Their star changes from each country's latest guide were marked on 5 Oct (w_changes.py, notes translated by w_notes.py); first editions aren't marked, and restaurants that lost their stars or closed in those guides weren't added (except two in the Netherlands). Prices (5–6 Oct) are listed in starred-bill-research/world/prices/*.txt (one line per restaurant: id | dinner | other menus from | wine | lunch | lunch wine | dinner source | lunch source | type; files named press* are press sources) and written into the restaurant files by w_prices_apply.py, with notes in each country's languages; mainland China's are Ctrip average spends (`dinnerType: spend`). About 100 still have none (49 in mainland China, the rest don't publish prices). The same day, p2_build.py (same folder) filled the gaps in Italy, Japan, Spain, Denmark and South Korea, reusing their existing region and city pages, and oj_merge.py added Kyoto, Osaka and Nara prices from OMAKASE (read in an omakaseje.com tab with the currency cookie set to JPY). Since then every restaurant on the MICHELIN Guide's world list is on the site; after a guide refresh, `_site/data/world.json` lists any starred restaurant that isn't.
- Copenhagen sits inside Denmark and includes the starred restaurants in Hellerup, Gentofte and Holte (the Michelin guide's "Copenhagen area"). Danish prices already include service and 25% VAT. Bangkok menus are often quoted "++" too (plus 10% service and 7% VAT): show the listed price and say "++" in the note. Singapore menus are priced "++": show the listed price, before the 10% service charge and 9% GST, as the Singapore page explains. Restaurants that closed since the previous guide stay listed with `status: closed`.
- Web addresses nest the whole chain, e.g. `/uk/england/london/`. A `district` sits inside a city (New York's five boroughs); restaurants there show "Neighbourhood, Borough" on the city page. Moving a page means adding its old address to the place's `redirectFrom`, which builds a forwarding page.
- Each country's currency must be in `content/currencies.json`.
- Head chefs (`chef`, `chefSource`) and dietary options (`diets`) come from the MICHELIN Guide's own data (Algolia index `prod-restaurants-en`), matched by `michelinId` once set. Refresh after guide releases with `scripts/michelin_details.py` (fetch, apply, review). Where Michelin names no chef, names were taken from restaurants' websites (`chefSource: site`, gathered by `scripts/chef_from_sites.py` and checked by hand), then from a web-search pass on 5 Oct 2026 (`chefSource: press`: recent reviews, interviews, hotel pages, and OMAKASE restaurant pages for Japan, read in an omakaseje.com tab with `/restaurants?keyword=<name>`). Restaurants still without one are listed by `michelin_details.py review`; Michelin's descriptions in other languages (e.g. `prod-restaurants-ja`, `-zh_CN`, `-fr`) occasionally name a chef the English one doesn't. A `manual` chef is never overwritten. Place pages show the chef under the name, leaf badges for vegetarian restaurants, vegetarian menus and vegan options, and a Dietary filter; chef names are searchable on place pages and the homepage.
- Sources used so far:
  - Stars, addresses and map positions: guide.michelin.com, extracted in the browser.
  - Google ratings: Places API (New) Text Search, sending `Referer: https://starredbill.com/` because the key only accepts set websites.
  - Prices: restaurant websites first, then recent reviews and booking sites.
  - Prices in the US: Tock pages (exploretock.com/<slug>) carry each menu's price in the page; OpenTable experience pages show prices including the service charge, so take the base amount (`minUnitAmount`) instead. OpenTable starts refusing after a few dozen quick fetches.
  - Prices in Japan: many top restaurants publish none. OMAKASE JapanEatinerary (omakaseje.com) restaurant pages list each course in yen, mostly including tax and service; read them with fetch() from inside an omakaseje.com browser tab, as it rate-limits heavy use. Pocket Concierge pages render in the browser after a moment. omakase.in and Tabelog show a Cloudflare check, so they can't be read, but search results quote their prices.

## Accounts
Free accounts let people keep their wishlist on every device and tick off restaurants they've been to ("been there").
- Supabase project `uvdaclbhskkukyngikhq` (London) holds sign-in and one table, `saved`: one row per person per restaurant (`wishlist`, `visited`, `visited_on`), keyed on the restaurant's file name. Its row-level security lets each person read and change only their own rows. `supabase/schema.sql` creates it (run in Supabase's SQL Editor); `delete_my_account()` lets someone delete themselves.
- `src/assets/account.js` loads the Supabase library from jsDelivr only when someone is signed in or signing in, signs in by an emailed 6-digit code (typed into the sign-in box, so it works inside the home-screen app, whose storage is separate from the browser's) or the email's link, or Google, and keeps the browser copies (`starredbill-wishlist`, `starredbill-visited` in localStorage) in step with the account. Pages keep reading the browser copies through `loadWishlist()` / `loadVisited()` and save through `setWishlist()` / `setVisited()` in common.js, which fire `sb:wishlist` / `sb:visited` events; `sb:account` fires when someone signs in or out.
- The first time a browser meets an account, anything saved while signed out is merged in; after that the account is the record. Changes wait in `starredbill-pending` until sent. Signing out clears both lists from that browser.
- Signed out, the wishlist works and stays in the browser; "been there" asks the person to sign up.
- The free Supabase plan pauses after about a week without activity, so `.github/workflows/supabase-keepalive.yml` asks the database one question every three days (it can also be run by hand from the Actions tab).
- `/account/` (account.html + account-page.js) shows stats, milestones, progress by destination, both lists, and download / sign out / delete. `/privacy/` (privacy.html + info-page.js) is the privacy notice in English and Chinese; update it if what's stored changes.
- In Supabase, Authentication › URL Configuration allows starredbill.com, www and localhost:8799. Google sign-in uses a Google Cloud OAuth client; sign-in emails go through Resend's SMTP from starredbill.com (set up 3 Oct 2026). Both sign-in emails (Magic Link and Confirm signup, under Authentication › Emails › Templates) use `supabase/email-template.html`, which shows the code (`{{ .Token }}`) above the link.

## Visit statistics
- Umami Cloud (cloud.umami.is, the owner's account) counts visits without cookies, so there's no cookie banner. Its script tag is in the `<head>` of every template in `src/` (place, home, account, privacy, 404), with `data-domains` set so local previews aren't counted.
- Clicks are sent as events by `track()` in common.js (and account.js for sign-ins): outbound (price source, Google Maps, other), share, language, currency, meal, search, near-me, compare, pick-step, pick-result, pick-share, pick-more, pick-restart, pick-lunch-hack, jump-to-map, wishlist, been-there, sign-in-opened, sign-in-link-sent, sign-in-code, sign-in-google, sign-in, account-created. - Google Search Console covers starredbill.com as a Domain property (DNS TXT at Namecheap), with the sitemap submitted.
- Google Analytics 4 (Measurement ID G-GE1KG403N4, `GA_ID` in common.js) runs alongside Umami, but only on starredbill.com and only after the visitor clicks Accept on the cookie banner (`renderCookieBar`, choice kept in localStorage `starredbill-consent`). The privacy page's "Change cookie choice" button reopens the banner. `track()` sends every event to both, with hyphens turned into underscores for Google (account-created also sends Google's `sign_up`).
- The owner's devices aren't counted: opening any page with `?notrack=1` sets Umami's `umami.disabled` in that browser, which also stops Google Analytics and hides the cookie banner (`NOTRACK` in common.js); `?notrack=0` undoes it.
- New accounts: Supabase › Authentication › Users lists every account; Umami's "account-created" event shows them next to visits.
