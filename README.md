# The Starred Bill

Compare dinner, lunch and wine pairing prices at Michelin-starred restaurants, city by city, in English and each country's main official language (Chinese, French, Japanese, Spanish, Korean…).

Live at https://starredbill.com

- Homepage with a world map, destination cards, search and your wishlist: https://starredbill.com/
- A page for every country, region and city, e.g. https://starredbill.com/uk/england/london/, https://starredbill.com/ireland/ or https://starredbill.com/taiwan/
- Add `?lang=en` for English. Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau offer Chinese (`?lang=zh`), Hong Kong and Macau also Cantonese (`?lang=yue`), France and Monaco French (`?lang=fr`), Japan Japanese (`?lang=ja`), Spain Spanish (`?lang=es`) Italy Italian (`?lang=it`) and South Korea Korean (`?lang=ko`), Denmark Danish (`?lang=da`), Sweden Swedish (`?lang=sv`), Iceland Icelandic (`?lang=is`) Andorra Catalan (`?lang=ca`) and Thailand Thai (`?lang=th`). Since 5 Oct 2026 also: Germany, Austria, Liechtenstein German (`?lang=de`); Switzerland German, French and Italian; Belgium Dutch (`?lang=nl`) and French; the Netherlands Dutch; Luxembourg French and German; Portugal and Brazil Portuguese (`?lang=pt`); mainland China Simplified Chinese (`?lang=zhs`); Norway Norwegian (`?lang=nb`); Finland Finnish (`?lang=fi`); Poland Polish (`?lang=pl`); Czechia Czech (`?lang=cs`); Hungary Hungarian (`?lang=hu`); Slovenia Slovenian (`?lang=sl`); Croatia Croatian (`?lang=hr`); Serbia Serbian in Cyrillic (`?lang=sr`); Greece Greek (`?lang=el`); Türkiye Turkish (`?lang=tr`); Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia Lithuanian, Latvian and Estonian (`?lang=lt`, `lv`, `et`); Malta Maltese (`?lang=mt`); Malaysia Malay (`?lang=ms`); the Philippines Filipino (`?lang=fil`); Vietnam Vietnamese (`?lang=vi`); Qatar and the UAE Arabic (`?lang=ar`), which turns the page right to left.

## How it fits together

```
content/                 the data (edit these, ideally through Pages CMS)
  restaurants/<country>/<restaurant>.json   one file per restaurant
  places/<place>.json    one file per country, region, city or collection
  guides/<guide>.json    one file per explainer article, published at /guides/<guide>/
  currencies.json        currency symbols and fallback exchange rates
  site.json              site-wide settings (when prices were last checked)
src/                     the design (templates, styles and scripts)
build.py                 turns content/ + src/ into the finished site in _site/
.pages.yml               the editing forms for Pages CMS
.github/workflows/       GitHub runs build.py on every push and publishes the result
```

Each restaurant is stored once, in its city. It then appears on that city's page and on every page above it (region, country), plus any collection that includes it. So a London restaurant shows on London, England and United Kingdom without being entered three times.

The Near me page (`/near-me/`) is built from the same restaurant files, so it needs no data of its own: every starred restaurant with a map position appears on it automatically. Link to it with `?q=` and a place to open it already searched, e.g. `/near-me/?q=Bath`.

The Help me pick page (`/pick/`) reads the same data: six questions (where, lunch or dinner, budget, stars, food, dietary needs), then a best match, a best value and a wildcard. Its "food mood" groups come from each restaurant's `cuisine` (`MOODS` in `src/assets/pick.js`), its value pick from `dinner`, `lunch`, `wine` and `lunchWine`, and its wildcard prefers a recent `change`. Link to it with `?w=` and a page's address to answer the first question, e.g. `/pick/?w=/uk/england/london/`.

## Places

Every file in `content/places/` has an `id` (lower-case, hyphens, used in the web address), a `type`, a `name` and an optional `nameZh`.

| type | needs | web address |
|---|---|---|
| `country` | `currency` (e.g. `"GBP"`) | `/uk/` |
| `region` | `parent`: a country or region id | `/uk/england/` |
| `city` | `parent`: a country or region id | `/uk/england/london/` |
| `district` | `parent`: a city id (New York's boroughs, London's neighbourhoods) | `/usa/new-york-state/new-york/manhattan/`, `/uk/england/london/mayfair/` |
| `group` | `includes`: a list of country, region or city ids | `/taiwan/southern-taiwan/` if all in one country, otherwise `/riviera/` |
| `cuisine` | `parent`: a city (or a city-state region or country), `cuisines`, `topic`; its `id` is the parent's and the cuisine | `/japan/tokyo/french/` for `tokyo-french` |

A region or city's address follows everything above it, so a Yorkshire region inside England would be `/uk/england/yorkshire/`, and York inside it `/uk/england/yorkshire/york/`. If a page moves, list its old address in `redirectFrom` (e.g. London has `["/uk/london/"]`) and old links keep working.

Restaurants don't need a city page of their own: one outside the cities we cover can sit in its region or country (`city` set to e.g. `england` or `ireland`), with the town and county in `area`, e.g. "Aughton, Lancashire".

Cuisine pages (9 Oct 2026) list one cuisine's starred restaurants in a city, for searches like "michelin star french restaurant tokyo" or "大阪 フレンチ ミシュラン". Make one only where the city has 5 or more starred restaurants of that cuisine; the build says when one falls below. `cuisines` lists the cuisine categories it covers, spelled as on the restaurants (`["Cantonese", "Shanghainese", "Zhejiang"]` for Chinese in Hong Kong; the build names any category its city doesn't have). `name` is its short label in breadcrumbs and links ("French", `nameJa` "フレンチ"), and `topic` what it lists as it reads mid-sentence ("French Michelin star restaurants", "Michelin star sushi restaurants"), with `topicJa`, `topicZh`, `topicYue`, `topicTh` and so on for its city's other languages. It offers its city's languages that have the cuisine-page wording (`cp…` in src/faq-words.json). Its title, heading, "In short" answer and questions are written from its restaurants; an optional `intro` replaces its city's. Its city's page links to it under its title and in "By cuisine", and the same cuisine's pages in other cities link to each other.

Groups are for areas that overlap the main structure, such as "the Riviera" (France and Italy) or "Northern England". Every place gets a page. One with no starred restaurants yet says so and links up to the nearest place that has some (e.g. Dorset links to England), so it's ready for its first star.

Optional `languages`, set on a country: the language buttons its pages offer (English and Chinese unless set). The rule is English, Chinese and the country's official language: Hong Kong and Macau add Cantonese, France French, Japan Japanese, Spain Spanish and Italy Italian. Any text field can have a `...Zh`, `...Yue` (Cantonese), `...Fr`, `...Ja` (Japanese), `...Es` (Spanish) or `...It` (Italian) version; Cantonese falls back to the Chinese text, and a missing translation falls back to English. French, Spanish and Italian place names also have `inSentenceFr`, `inSentenceEs` and `inSentenceIt`, the name with its preposition ("à Paris", "en el País Vasco", "nel Lazio"); Spanish defaults to "en" + the name, and Italian to "a" + a city or "in" + a country or region. The languages added on 5 Oct 2026 use the suffixes `Zhs`, `De`, `Nl`, `Pt`, `Nb`, `Fi`, `Pl`, `Cs`, `Hu`, `Sl`, `Hr`, `Sr`, `El`, `Tr`, `Lt`, `Lv`, `Et`, `Mt`, `Ms`, `Fil`, `Vi` and `Ar` (e.g. `nameDe`, `introZhs`, `dinnerNoteFil`, `areaAr`). German, Dutch, Portuguese, Norwegian, Malay, Filipino, Vietnamese and Arabic ("في الدوحة") build the mid-sentence name from a preposition ("in München", "no Porto" set by hand); the others decline names, so every place offering them must set it (`inSentenceFi` "Helsingissä", `inSentencePl` "w Polsce"), and the build says which are missing.

Optional `plural: true`, English only: the name reads as plural ("Napa and Sonoma", "the Channel Islands", "the Canary Islands"), so the "In short" answer says "have" rather than "has". That answer also starts with `inSentence` where it begins with "the" ("The UK has…").

Optional `title`, `description` and `lead`, English only and never passed down: a place's own page title (up to 61 characters), search-result snippet (up to 155) and opening sentence, replacing the generated ones. Use them for pages that answer a question first, e.g. Boston's "Michelin Star Restaurants in Boston: Are There Any? ({year})" (Brief 15), and refresh them after each guide. `{year}` in a title becomes the year of its MICHELIN Guide's latest edition, as in generated titles.

Optional `localNote`, English only and never passed down: a hand-written note of 150 to 250 words on booking there, shown as a "Before you book" section after the new stars. It's a list of `{"q": …, "a": …}`: the first question is the section's heading ("How do you book a Michelin star restaurant in NYC?"), the others (when tables open, what to wear, tipping) sit under it, and all of them join the page's FAQ structured data, replacing its general "How far ahead should you book?" question. In an answer, `{r:le-bernardin}` names a restaurant by its file name and links to it. `localNoteChecked` is the day it was last checked against the restaurants' websites (`2026-10-09`), shown under it. Write it from what the restaurants' own sites say, and recheck it every few months.

Optional `areasNote` (and `areasNoteDe`, `areasNoteFr`… in the page's other languages), never passed down: one hand-written sentence under "Starred restaurants, area by area", for linking a page inside with the words people search for it (`"For the state capital on its own, see our page of {p:stuttgart|Michelin star restaurants in Stuttgart}…"`). `{p:<place>|words}` links a place by its file name, `{r:<restaurant>|words}` a restaurant (its own page, else its row; Texas → Tatsu Dallas). Each language's page shows only its own note, never the English one.

Optional text, set on a country and used by everything inside it unless a region or city sets its own: `inSentence` (the name mid-sentence, e.g. "the UK"), `intro`, `serviceText`, `sourcesText`, `starsText`, each with a `...Zh` version for Chinese. `michelinGuideUrl` is that guide's starred restaurants on guide.michelin.com (`https://guide.michelin.com/en/<country code>/restaurants/all-starred`, or `/us/en/<state>/restaurants/all-starred` for a US state's guide); the first mention of the MICHELIN Guide in `starsText` (in each language) links to it.

## Restaurants

One file per restaurant in `content/restaurants/<country>/`. The file name is its id (e.g. `the-ledbury.json`) and is used for wishlists, so don't rename it once published.

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `name`, `nameZh`, `nameJa`, `nameZhs` | restaurant name (on Chinese pages, a restaurant with only `nameJa` shows it under the English name; `nameZhs` is the Simplified Chinese name for mainland China, from the MICHELIN Guide) |
| `city` | id of its city in `content/places/` |
| `area`, `areaZh`, `areaJa`, `address`, `addressZh`, `addressJa` | neighbourhood and full address |
| `stars` | 1, 2 or 3 |
| `starSource`, `starSourceType`, `starSourceDate` | where the stars come from (13 Oct 2026): a link, its kind (`michelin-press`: the MICHELIN Guide's press release or press kit; `news`: news coverage; `restaurant`: the restaurant's own announcement) and the day it was published (`2026-02-09`). Never a guide.michelin.com page, whose terms forbid copying it (the build refuses one). Set it when a ceremony changes a restaurant's stars. Without it, the restaurant page cites the guide's full selection from Michelin's press release where its ceremony has a `selection` (see "Ceremony dates"), else its MICHELIN Guide page, where the stars were taken from before 13 Oct 2026. `scripts/site_audit.py` counts those still resting on the guide's website (`stars_from_guide_site`) |
| `cuisine` | Our own cuisine category (15 Oct 2026), one of `CUISINE_CATEGORIES` in build.py: Modern European, Modern American or Modern (modern cooking in Europe, the Americas, elsewhere), Creative, Classic, Regional, Farm to table, a country's or region's cooking (French, Cantonese, Sichuan…), or what's on the plate (Sushi, Tempura, Yakitori, Seafood, Steak and grill, Barbecue, Vegetarian, Vegan, Street food). | `wine` | cheapest wine pairing, as a number |

Set only on positive evidence |
| `taxIncluded` | `true`, with `serviceIncluded`, when the price includes the sales tax as well as the tip (Sushi Nishinokaze: tax and service included, no tips). The bills then add nothing and receipts say "service included" |
| `source`, `sourceType` | link to where the price came from; `site`, `press`, `member` or `none`. `member` is a member's report ("Report a price or change"), checked against the restaurant's own site before use; `source` can then be empty |
| `sourceDate`, `lunchSourceDate` | for a `member` price, the month the member saw it (`2026-10`); shown as "Member's report, Oct 2026". Never the member's name |
| `lunch`, `lunchType`, `lunchNote`, `lunchNoteZh`, `lunchWine`, `lunchSource`, `lunchSourceType` | the same for lunch |
| `noLunch` | `true` when there's no lunch service |
| `priceVerifiedAt`, `lunchVerifiedAt` | the last day someone genuinely checked the dinner (with its wine) or lunch price at its source (`2026-10-27`), even when it hadn't changed. Set only by a real check: `python3 scripts/prices.py verify <id>` (or `set` when the price changed). Restaurant pages say "Price checked 9 Oct 2026" from it. See "Price dates and provenance" |
| `priceChangedAt`, `lunchChangedAt` | the last day our record of that price changed (or was first entered), worked out from git by `python3 scripts/price_dates.py --write`. Never shown as a check: pages say "recorded Oct 2026 (not rechecked since)" when there's no `…VerifiedAt` |
| `sourceKind`, `lunchSourceKind` | optional: what kind of source the price came from (`restaurant`, `booking`, `guide`, `press`, `member` or `none`), where the link's address doesn't say it (`source_kind()` works it out otherwise) |

| `status`, `formerStars`, `statusNote`, `statusNoteZh` | only for restaurants no longer starred: `lost`, `closed` or `changed` |

| `notice`, `noticeZh` | a current notice, e.g. temporarily closed; shows as a "Temporarily closed" badge |

| `website`, `lat`, `lng`, `placeId` | website, map position and Google place id (for the photo, the Reviews link and Google's place card; restaurants Google has no listing of its own for are in `content/no-place-ids.json`, with the day they were looked up) |
| `chef`, `chefSource` | head chef, and where the name came from: `site` (the restaurant's website), `press` (recent reviews, interviews, hotel or booking pages such as OMAKASE, checked by hand), `manual` (never overwritten) or `michelin` (taken from the MICHELIN Guide's search index before 13 Oct 2026; replace with one of the others when rechecked, never add new ones) |
| `diets` | dietary options: any of `vegetarian-only`, `vegetarian-menu`, `vegetarian`, `vegan`, `gluten-free`, `halal`, `kosher`. Most were taken from the MICHELIN Guide's search index before 13 Oct 2026; new ones come from the restaurant's own menus and website, with `dietsManual` |

| `michelinUrl` | the restaurant's page on guide.michelin.com, a link for citing it (the ↗ beside its name in guide tables, its restaurant page's Sources line). Optional: restaurants on the site before 13 Oct 2026 have theirs in `content/michelin-links.json` (file name → path after `https://guide.michelin.com/en`), kept from the retired world list; the field wins over that |
| `michelinId` | the restaurant's record id in the MICHELIN Guide's search index, left from the retired refresh; nothing reads it |

Leave out any field you don't have.

Restaurants listed in `RESTAURANT_PAGES` (build.py) also get a page of their own at `/restaurants/<file name>/`, built from the same fields: the prices as a till receipt and a bill with service, the stars, chef, cuisine, dietary options, Google rating, how the dinner price compares with the same stars nearby, the starred restaurants within 5 km and a FAQ. Destination pages and guide tables link to it. Its link-preview picture is its till receipt, drawn by `python3 scripts/restaurant_images.py` on the Mac into `src/og/restaurants/<id>.png` (run it again after changing a restaurant page's prices).

Every other starred restaurant with a price gets a simpler page from the same template (no hand-researched menus or booking rules, no link-preview picture of its own, and its Google photo shown only when "Show photos" is tapped), released in waves by `content/restaurant-pages.json`: `waves` is a list of `{from, label, ids}`, where `from` is the day the wave goes live (the first build on or after it publishes it) and `ids` its restaurants' file names. A restaurant appears in one wave at most, and never in a wave and `RESTAURANT_PAGES` both. `python3 scripts/restaurant_waves.py list` shows the waves; `add` puts restaurants that qualify later (a new star or a new price; open, with no `notice`) into the next wave. The build names any that qualify but aren't in a wave.

Restaurant pages have no separate FAQ: the questions people search are the page's own headings, each answered straight underneath, and the structured data lists them (10 at most). These optional fields appear only on a restaurant's own page; each part of the page is left out until they're filled in (Le Bernardin has them all):

Nothing is taken from the MICHELIN Guide's website or search index any more (13 Oct 2026; its terms of use forbid copying its pages by robot or by hand). The scripts that did so (`michelin_details.py`, `michelin_world.js`, `receive.py`, and the research pipelines listed in starred-bill-research/RETIRED-MICHELIN.md) are retired, and `content/world-starred.json` is gone: the homepage's country counts and map come from our own files. Data taken from the guide before then (stars, chefs marked `michelin`, dietary options without `dietsManual`, the hours in `content/opening-hours.json`, addresses, positions and cuisine labels) is being re-sourced restaurant by restaurant from Michelin's press releases, the restaurants' own websites and OpenStreetMap; `scripts/site_audit.py` counts what's left. Chefs are added with `python3 scripts/chefs.py add FILE` (`missing` lists the starred restaurants without one), after `scripts/chef_from_sites.py` has gathered candidates from restaurants' own websites.

Opening hours live in `content/opening-hours.json`, taken from the MICHELIN Guide by a script retired on 13 Oct 2026 (so it's no longer refreshed; replace its hours with the restaurants' own as they're rechecked): `checked` (the day they were fetched) and `hours`, one string per restaurant file name, Monday first, days split by `;`, each day's sittings by `,`, e.g. `";1200-1430,1800-2230;…"` (closed on Monday). The guide gives none for all of Japan, most of mainland China and many hotel dining rooms, so `content/opening-hours-sites.json` (same shape, plus `sources`: per restaurant `url`, the page the hours were read on, and `type`: `site` the restaurant's own website, `hotel` its hotel's, `other` anything else such as a booking, tourism or Trip.com page; `daysOnly`, those whose page gave the days and meals but not the times, whose placeholder times are never shown; `seasons`, a sentence saying when a seasonal restaurant opens, shown beside its hours; and `none`, the starred restaurants whose own site, hotel and booking pages give no usable hours, each `{"checked": "2026-10-19", "note": "why"}`, which `scripts/site_audit.py` counts as `no_hours_anywhere` rather than `no_hours` for 120 days after `checked`) holds hours read by hand from those pages (308 on 12 Oct 2026, 482 more on 13 Oct 2026). Restaurant pages credit that page in their Sources line. Google's hours (`content/opening-hours-google.json`, from `scripts/google_hours.py`) filled that gap until 13 Oct 2026, when both were deleted: Google's Maps Platform terms allow keeping only place IDs (and positions for 30 days), not other Places details, so don't store hours or other details fetched from Google (the stored `rating` and `reviews` break the same rule; the owner has put that off, to-do item legal-google-ratings-terms). The MICHELIN Guide's hours win, except for the restaurants listed in that file's `replacesGuide`, whose guide hours are plainly wrong (BingSheng Private Kitchen on Tianhe East Road: 11:00–04:00 in the guide, 11:00–14:00 and 17:00–22:00 on Trip.com; 12 Oct 2026). The guide often records only a day's first sitting, so the site trusts which days a restaurant opens, but shows its times only for days with two sittings or a dinner one; the websites' times (except `daysOnly`) are always shown. Restaurant pages say which source their hours came from. Near me's "Open on" filter reads them (`/data/hours.json`), and restaurant pages show them under "Opening hours" (or "Open", days only). `scripts/chef_from_sites.py` gathers chef mentions from restaurants' own websites for checking by hand.

### Price dates and provenance

Since 27 Oct 2026 every recorded price can say where it came from and how fresh it is, so we can tell later which figures
are a third party's and which are our own (for a data licence or API) and never claim a check we didn't make:

- **Source** (`source`, `lunchSource`, `menusSource`): the link the price was read on.
- **Kind of source** (`source_kind()` in build.py): `restaurant` (its own website, `sourceType: site`), `booking` (a booking
  platform or agent: Resy, Tock, OpenTable, TheFork, TableCheck, OMAKASE…, `BOOKING_HOSTS`), `guide` (another restaurant
  guide, rating or listing site: Gault&Millau, Tabelog, Ctrip, OpenRice…, `GUIDE_HOSTS`), `press` (reviews, news,
  blogs), `member` (a member's report, checked) or `none`. Set `sourceKind`/`lunchSourceKind` only where the address misleads.
- **Checked** (`priceVerifiedAt`, `lunchVerifiedAt`; `menusChecked` for `menus`): the last day someone read the source and
  confirmed the price. Record every check, even when nothing changed: `python3 scripts/prices.py verify <id> [dinner|lunch|both]`.
- **Changed** (`priceChangedAt`, `lunchChangedAt`): the last day our record changed, from git (`scripts/price_dates.py`).
  The first values (27 Oct 2026) were worked out from the history; the 352 dinner and 146 lunch prices that matched menus
  read on `menusChecked` got that day as their check.
- **As published or derived** (`priceDerived`, `lunchDerived`): absent means the figure is as the source gave it; a note
  says how we worked it out otherwise. Our own sums (service, tax and tips, conversions, medians, estimated wine) are never
  stored as prices.

`python3 scripts/prices.py set <id> dinner 380 --source <url> --type site` records a new price: it sets the changed and
checked days and keeps the old dinner price in `priceHistory`. `price_records()` in build.py gives a restaurant's prices
in one shape with all of this, for any later export. Quarterly snapshots (`python3 build.py --snapshot 2027-Q1`, into
`content/price-snapshots/`) freeze them for like-for-like comparisons: see `docs/price-index-method.md`.

### The Price Index

`/guides/michelin-price-index/` is The Starred Bill Michelin Price Index: a guide whose figures all come from one frozen
quarterly snapshot, never from the live restaurant files. An edition is two files:

- `content/price-snapshots/<quarter>.json`, the snapshot (`python3 build.py --snapshot 2027-Q1`);
- `content/price-index/<quarter>.json`, what the snapshot doesn't hold, saved the same day by
  `python3 build.py --index-edition 2027-Q1`: `rates` (each currency's `perUSD` on the snapshot's `rateDate`, read from
  `currencies.json` at the snapshot's commit), `service` (each country's usual service rule then) and `cities` (which
  restaurants count under each city with 10 or more, as the most-stars guide groups them). Neither is ever rewritten,
  except for what the check before publication adds to the edition file (never to the snapshot):
  - `corrections`: snapshot records found to have been wrong when taken (`id`, `meal`, and `price`, `sourceKind`, `wine`
    or `remove`, with `error` in plain words, `evidence` and `checked`), listed on the page; a price that has simply
    changed since is not corrected, as the edition describes the quarter;
  - `confirmed`: ids whose set lunch was checked and may be compared (lunches under a quarter of dinner, or equal to it,
    are otherwise held back);
  - `validated` / `unconfirmed`: "<id>:<meal>" confirmed at source in that check, or not settled either way (shown as
    "checked" / "not rechecked" in the tables); `excluded`: "<id>:<meal>" kept out of the rankings until verified, with why;
  - `notes`: "<id>:<meal>" → a few words on a ranked menu (courses, days, minimum guests);
  - `validation`: the check's day and counts, and `sameSample` (the same-price lunch sample: counts, how it was chosen,
    the pattern found), quoted on the page without scaling the sample up to the whole list.
  The evidence for 2026-Q4 is in starred-bill-research/price-checks/index-q4 (results files and apply.py).

The guide's `priceIndex` field names the edition it shows (`"2026-Q4"`). Its body is ordinary guide text with figures
(`{{piMedian1}}`, `{{piCitiesN}}`, `{{piCheapCity}}`, `{{piVerifiedShare}}`…) and blocks (`{{table:pi-headlines}}`,
`pi-strip`, `pi-stars`, `pi-city-chart`, `pi-cheap-city`, `pi-dear-city`, `pi-cities`, `pi-four`, `pi-countries`,
`pi-cheap-1/2/3`, `pi-cheap-3-line`, `pi-lunch-line`, `pi-lunch`, `pi-savings`, `pi-data`, `pi-method`, `pi-cite`) from
`price_index_stats()`/`price_index_blocks()` in build.py. Settings: `PI_MIN` (10 menus before a median is published),
`PI_TOP`, `PI_ODD_LUNCH`, `PI_CITIES`. The page also carries Dataset structured data and its own link-preview picture
(`src/og/michelin-price-index.png`, `python3 scripts/og_images.py michelin-price-index`). A new edition: take the snapshot,
make the edition file, change `priceIndex` (and `published`/`updated`, and the picture's quarter), reread the page's prose.

Its figures are `{{mostCity1}}`–`{{mostCity5}}` (with `N`, `Stars`, `Country`, `Split` and `3` on the end), `{{mostStarsCity}}`/`{{mostStarsCityN}}` (most stars), `{{most3City}}`/`{{most3CityN}}` and `{{most3City2}}`/`{{most3City2N}}` (most three-star restaurants), `{{mostCityBeats}}` (countries with fewer starred restaurants than the top city), `{{mostCities}}` and `{{mostCities10}}` (towns and cities with a star, and with ten or more), `{{mostCountryStars}}`/`{{mostSecondCountryStars}}`, `{{mostStarsCountry1}}`–`3` (countries by total stars, with `N`), `{{mostUS1}}`–`{{mostUS4}}` (with `N`, `Stars`, `3`), `{{mostPer1}}`–`3` (with `N`, per million people), `{{mostPerStar}}` (median price per star worldwide), `{{mostCheapStar}}`/`{{mostCheapStarUSD}}` and `{{mostDearStar}}`/`{{mostDearStarUSD}}` (cheapest and dearest star among the table's cities), and per city or country id, hyphens as underscores, `{{inCity_nara}}` (starred restaurants), `{{perStar_kyoto}}` and `{{menu_new_york}}` (US dollars) and `{{perM_france}}` (per million people).

For the closures guide (UK Michelin star restaurants that closed or lost their stars), every country and year with no-longer-starred restaurants on record gets two tables: `{{table:gone-uk-2026}}` lists those that closed, changed or lost their stars that year, by when it happened (`statusDate`), newest first, with the stars each held and its `statusNote`; `{{table:left-uk-2026}}` lists what that year's guide dropped or demoted (`changeDate`, with `status` or `change: down`), most stars first, saying whether each is still open. Their figures, with the country id and year on the end (hyphens as underscores): `{{goneN_uk_2026}}` and `{{goneClosed_uk_2026}}`, `{{leftN_uk_2026}}`, `{{leftClosed_uk_2026}}` and `{{leftOpen_uk_2026}}`, and `{{sinceN_uk_2026}}`/`{{sinceClosed_uk_2026}}` (gone that year but after its guide, so still to be dropped). A restaurant that closes between guides keeps its `changeDate` empty until the next guide leaves it out.

A link written `<a data-guide="green-michelin-star">…</a>` becomes a link once that guide exists, and plain text until then.

### Translated guides

A guide's translation is `content/guide-translations/<language>/<id>.json` (editable in Pages CMS as "Guide translations"), published at `/<language>/guides/<id>/`, e.g. `/de/guides/what-is-a-michelin-star/`. It keeps the English guide's file name and picture. The English guide and its translations name each other with hreflang links and language buttons, a visitor who reads one of those languages is sent to it (as on destination pages), and the Guides page shows "Also in: Deutsch · Español…" under that guide. Destination pages in a language whose "What is a Michelin star?" translation exists link to it from their footer. Languages so far: German (`de`), Spanish (`es`), Italian (`it`), Portuguese (`pt`, written for Brazil, `locale` pt-BR), Polish (`pl`), Japanese (`ja`, 23 Oct 2026, with a section on Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka and Nara) and French (`fr`, 23 Oct 2026, with a section on France, Monaco, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland and Québec); a new one needs its wording in `GUIDE_WORDS` in build.py first (date lines, FAQ and Sources headings, how it writes numbers, US dollars and month names, local prices where they're written their own way (`money`: "31,460円"), and country names for the figures).

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `id` | the English guide's file name |
| `h1`, `title`, `description`, `summary`, `imageAlt`, `published`, `updated`, `keywords`, `body`, `faq` | as in the English guide, in the language (description up to 160 characters) |
| `locale` | optional regional language for the article, e.g. `pt-BR` |
| `sources` | optional extra sources for facts only the translation has; the English guide's sources are listed too |

Figures work as in the English guide but come out the language's way: `{{n3}}` as "161", `{{total}}` as "3.885" in German, `{{price1}}` as "175 US$" (German, Spanish), "175 USD" (Italian, Polish) or "US$ 175" (Portuguese), `{{range1}}` as "140–215 US$", `{{checked}}` as "Oktober 2026", and country names (`{{topCountry}}`…) in the language, without an article, so write them into lists like "Frankreich (646)". Tables (`{{table:…}}`) aren't available. A translation can also quote a destination's own figures with `{{in:<place id>:<field>}}`: `n` (starred restaurants), `n1`, `n2`, `n3`, `stars` (stars between them), `median` (the typical dinner tasting menu, local currency), `from` and `fromName` (the cheapest and where) and `three` (its three-star restaurants, named). Polish can't put a noun after a number without changing its ending, so Polish text puts figures after a label ("Restauracji z gwiazdką Michelin jest w Polsce 11"). A link to a guide (`data-guide`) opens its translation in that language, or the English guide with a small EN tag; a link to a destination page (`href="/germany/"`) opens its version in that language where it has one.

The ceremony dates guide (`/guides/michelin-guide-ceremony-dates/`) reads `content/ceremonies.json`, one entry per MICHELIN Guide (editable in Pages CMS as "Ceremony dates"):

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `id` | a short name, e.g. `great-britain-ireland`; the guide's row is `#cer-<id>` |
| `name` | e.g. "MICHELIN Guide Great Britain & Ireland" |
| `places` | the place ids it covers (and everything inside them). The build lists any country with starred restaurants that no guide covers |
| `show` | optional: the places to name and link instead, e.g. the cities rather than the states |
| `guide`, `showName`, `continent` | for a guide with no place page yet (South Australia): one of our guides to link, the name to show, and its continent |
| `usual` | the month(s) it's usually held, read after "usually in" |
| `ceremonies` | past ceremonies, newest first: `date` (`2026-02-09`), `where` (left out when unknown), `online` (true when the stars were published online with no ceremony) and `source` (a link), plus `selection` (a link to the guide's full list of starred restaurants in Michelin's press release or press kit, never guide.michelin.com; set it only once our files for that guide have been checked against it, and the restaurant pages without their own `starSource` then cite it for their stars) and `edition` where the guide is named for the next year (Italy's November 2026 ceremony launches the 2027 guide; leave it out when it's the ceremony's own year). From the ceremony day on, destination and restaurant page titles carry that edition's year (`title_year()` in build.py): a page covered by several guides follows the one most of its restaurants are in, and no page shows a year older than site.json's `updated` |
| `next` | the next ceremony once Michelin announces it (same fields). When its day comes the page treats it as the latest, and the build asks for it to be moved into `ceremonies` |
| `expected` | optional year-month the next is due, when that isn't a year after the last (Greece) |
| `starsUpdated` | the day our restaurant files caught up with this guide. While its latest ceremony is newer, the page shows "We're updating our pages" and every build prints a reminder. Setting it (and pushing) also sends the star emails to members who asked for them (scripts/star_alerts.py), so set it only once the guide's restaurants are all updated |
| `checked` | the month we last looked for a newer date |
| `note` | an optional sentence shown under the guide's name |
| `nameFr`, `usualDe`, `noteJa`, `whereIt`… | optional translations for the "When the stars are announced" card on destination pages in that language (suffix as in place files: Fr, Es, It, De, Ja, Nl, Pt, Zh, Zhs, Ar…; Cantonese reads the Chinese ones, `nameYue` only where its name differs, as Hong Kong's 米芝蓮). `name` the guide's name in that language ("Guide MICHELIN Deutschland"); `usual` with its preposition ("en mars", "im Juni", "a novembre", "entre abril y julio", "w maju", "kesäkuussa"; Chinese, Japanese and Korean without: "9月か10月", "3월"); `note` the note; `where` (inside a ceremony or `next`) the venue, where its English name won't do. Left out, the page uses the English name and venue and skips the usual month and note |
| `results` | optional: gives the guide a results page at `/guides/<page>/`. `page` (starts `michelin-guide-`, never changes), `short` (the name in the title, e.g. "UK & Ireland"), `area` (as a sentence names it, e.g. "the UK and Ireland"), `lang` (`en-GB` for the UK, Ireland, Singapore and the UAE, else `en-US`), `plural` (true when the area takes "have"), `published`, and extra `keywords` |

**Results pages** (`/guides/michelin-guide-singapore/` and the rest, for guides with `results`): built by the build, not written by hand. The latest edition our files hold (the newest ceremony on or before `starsUpdated`) is worked out from the restaurants in the guide's `places`: `change` new/up/down with a `changeDate` from that ceremony's month on, and restaurants with a `status` and such a `changeDate` (they left the guide). Restaurants with a `status` but no `changeDate` show as "Closed since" that edition. Earlier editions come from `content/results.json`, which `python3 build.py --freeze <guide id>` writes (restaurant file names and star counts per edition); run it on ceremony day before changing any restaurant, so last year's results stay on the page.

Its tables are `{{table:ceremonies}}` (every guide by continent), `{{table:ceremonies-next}}` (announced dates in order, then guides whose turn comes within `CEREMONY_AHEAD_DAYS` with last year's date), `{{table:ceremonies-recent}}` (the last `CEREMONY_RECENT_DAYS`, with whether our pages have caught up) and `{{table:ceremonies-calendar}}` (the year at a glance). Its figures are `{{cerGuides}}`, `{{cerNext}}`, `{{cerNextDate}}`, `{{cerNextWhere}}`, `{{cerBusy}}` (the busiest months) and `{{cerSay_<id>}}`, a sentence on a guide's next (or last) ceremony with hyphens as underscores, e.g. `{{cerSay_northeast_cities}}`.

## News

Short, dated stories about Michelin-starred restaurants at `/news/<file name>/`, one JSON file per story in `content/news/` (Pages CMS "News"), listed newest first at `/news/` (with the next ceremonies beside them) and in the RSS feed at `/news/feed.xml` (the latest 30, with the full story). Each story is a NewsArticle in its structured data, in the sitemap, linked from the homepage's "What's new" (the latest three), from English destination pages' "New Michelin stars" section ("In the news", stories about that place or anywhere inside it) and from restaurant pages it names ("… in the news"). Aim for one or two a week plus ceremony nights; the build reminds you when the newest is more than `NEWS_STALE_DAYS` (10) days old.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `id` | The file name, which is the page's address. Never change it once published. |
| `h1` | The headline. |
| `title` | The title in search results, up to 60 characters. |
| `description` | The description in search results, up to 155 characters. |
| `summary` | One or two sentences for the News page and the feed. |
| `kind` | `stars` (new and lost stars), `ceremony` (coming up), `closure`, `opening`, `chef` (chef moves), `prices`, `rankings` (50 Best, La Liste, OAD) or `data` (our own figures). Shown above the headline. |
| `published` | The day it went up, like 2026-10-14. |
| `updated` | Optional: the day it was last changed, if it was. |
| `written` | Only for a story written up after the news broke: the day it went on the site. `published` is then the day the news broke (which dates and orders it); the page shows "Written up …" beside it, and search engines, the feed and the sitemap get `written` as its publication date. |
| `imageAlt` | What the featured picture shows, in one plain sentence. The picture is `src/img/news/<id>.jpg` (with `-card` and `-og`), in the news style of docs/image-style.md, sized by `python3 scripts/guide_images.py --news <id>`. |
| `lang` | `en-GB` (the default) or `en-US` for US spelling and dates. |
| `places` | Optional: place ids the story is about (its restaurants' towns count anyway). The first gives the story its link-preview picture. |
| `body` | The story, as HTML. Answer first: what happened, where and when, in the first 40–50 words. `<a data-r="le-bernardin">` links a restaurant (its own page, else its row on its town's page), `<a data-place="london">` a destination page, `<a data-guide="michelin-guide-texas">` a guide (plain text until it exists) and `<a data-news="…">` another story. Figures like `{{dinner:fabrik}}` work as in guides. Link each page once, never at the start of a sentence. |
| `sources` | Required, as for guides: Michelin's press releases (not the MICHELIN Guide's website, which the build refuses), news coverage and restaurants' own announcements. |
| `sourcesNote` | Optional sentence under the sources, e.g. that the counts are our own. |

## Bib Gourmand

The MICHELIN Guide's Bib Gourmands (good food at a moderate price), starting with Taiwan (24 Oct 2026). They are kept apart from the starred restaurants: one file each in `content/bib/<country>/` (Pages CMS "Bib Gourmand"), never in `content/restaurants/`, so no star count, map, Near me, Help me pick or email includes them. Each country with a list gets a page at `/<country>/bib-gourmand/` (every city's list, in short, with full tables for cities without a destination page, e.g. New Taipei) and one per city that has a destination page (`/taiwan/taichung/bib-gourmand/`), in each language its destination pages offer, linked from those destination pages under their intro. The list comes from Michelin's press release or news coverage, never the MICHELIN Guide's website; recheck it after each year's announcement (Taiwan's: July or August, a week before the stars).

`content/bib-gourmand.json` holds each country's list:

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `guide` / `guideZh` | The guide's name, e.g. MICHELIN Guide Taiwan. |
| `edition` | The year of the list, e.g. 2026. |
| `announced` | The day the list came out, like 2026-07-14. |
| `checked` | The month the prices were checked, like 2026-10. |
| `new` | How many are new this year; the build checks it against the files marked `change: "new"`. |
| `whatIs` / `whatIsZh` | The country page's answer to "What is a Bib Gourmand?" (the local price level). |
| `next` / `nextZh` | When the next list is due. |
| `cities` | Each list city: `id`, `name`/`nameZh`, `place` (its destination page's id, if it has one), `count` (how many the list gives it; the build checks the files add up) and an optional `note`. |
| `sources` | The list's sources (`title`, `publisher`, `url`, `date`), as for guides. |

Each restaurant's file (the file name is its id, so don't rename one once published):

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `name` / `nameZh` | Its name: the English one it or English-language press uses, else Hanyu Pinyin; and the local name. |
| `city` | One of the list's city ids in bib-gourmand.json. |

| `address` / `addressZh` | Its address, for the Google Maps link and the structured data. |

## Saved searches

Members' saved searches live in Supabase (`saved_searches`, set up by `supabase/saved-searches.sql`), not in `content/`. Each row's `query` holds only the keys it uses:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `p` | the page it was saved on: a place id (`tokyo`, `tokyo-french`, `mayfair`) or, from Help me pick, its address; blank for anywhere |
| `s` | star levels, e.g. `[2]` or `[2, 3]` |
| `k` | a cuisine category (`Japanese`), from a destination page's Cuisine filter |
| `f` | Help me pick's food moods (`japanese`, `veg`…; `MOODS` in pick.js and scripts/saved_searches.py, kept in step) |
| `d` | a dietary need: `vegetarian`, `vegan`, `gluten-free`, `halal` or `kosher` |
| `a` | a neighbourhood, the first part of a restaurant's `area` (`Ginza`) |
| `b`, `c`, `m`, `w` | a price limit: at most `b` per person in currency `c`, for meal `m` (`dinner`, `lunch`, or `value`: whichever is cheaper), with a wine pairing when `w` is true (else about 60% of the menu is allowed for one) |

`label` is its plain-English name and `page` the address that shows it again (destination pages read `?stars=&cuisine=&diet=&area=&meal=&max=&cur=&wine=`). After pushes that change restaurants, and every morning, `.github/workflows/saved-searches.yml` builds the site and runs `scripts/saved_searches.py`, which emails a member when a restaurant newly matches one of their searches because its own file changed (a new star, a lower or newly listed price or pairing, a new dietary option or cuisine category, or a restaurant new to the site), at most once a day. Preview one with `python3 scripts/saved_searches.py --preview out/ --query '{"p": "london", "d": "vegan"}' --since HEAD~50`.

## Members' scores

Members who have ticked a restaurant as "been there" can score it from 1 to 5 in their diary (the `score` column of their own row in Supabase's `saved` table, set up by `supabase/scores.sql`; unticking "been there" clears it). Each morning `.github/workflows/member-scores.yml` runs `scripts/member_scores.py`, which asks Supabase's `member_scores()` for each restaurant's average and count, only where five or more members have scored it (accounts under a day old and members listed in `score_excluded` left out), and writes them into `content/member-scores.json`, committing it only when something changed. Don't edit that file by hand:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `updated` | the day the averages last changed |
| `scores` | per restaurant file name, `[average, count]`, e.g. `"le-bernardin": [4.63, 12]` |

The build gives those restaurants `memberScore` (the average to one decimal and the count), shown as "Members: 4.6 (12)" in the reviews column on destination rows and map pop-ups, and as "Members' score" on restaurant pages. It never goes in the structured data. `member_score_checks()` (secret key only) lists accounts whose scoring looks unusual (ten or more scores in a day, or five or more all the same) for the weekly member-reports review; anyone found faking scores is added to `score_excluded` by hand.

## Editing

The easiest way is Pages CMS: go to https://app.pagescms.org, sign in with GitHub and open **starred-bill**. Restaurants, Places, Guides, Currencies and Site settings each have a form; click **Save** and the site updates within a couple of minutes.

You can also edit a file on GitHub (pencil icon) and click **Commit changes**. Either way GitHub rebuilds the site and it's live within a couple of minutes. If something in the data is wrong, such as a misspelt city id or a price typed as `"£95"`, the build stops, the live site stays as it was, and the **Actions** tab says exactly what to fix.
