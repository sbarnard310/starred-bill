# The Starred Bill: notes for Claude

Compares dinner, lunch and wine pairing prices at Michelin-starred restaurants, with a page per country, region and city. Live at https://starredbill.com (GitHub repo sbarnard310/starred-bill). Each country's pages offer English plus its main official language (Chinese and Cantonese for Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau, Chinese also for Singapore at the owner's request; Simplified Chinese for mainland China; French, Japanese, Spanish, Italian, Korean, Danish, Swedish, Icelandic, Catalan, Thai, German, Dutch, Portuguese, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Slovenian, Croatian, Serbian, Greek, Turkish, Lithuanian, Latvian, Estonian, Maltese, Malay, Filipino, Vietnamese, Arabic for Qatar and the UAE; Switzerland has German, French and Italian, Belgium Dutch and French, Luxembourg French and German, all at the owner's choice on 5 Oct 2026); the homepage, account and privacy pages are English only. README.md documents every data field. Keep it in step when fields change.

## How it's built
- `content/` is the data, edited by the owner in Pages CMS (app.pagescms.org) or on GitHub. There is one JSON file per restaurant in `content/restaurants/<country>/`, one per place in `content/places/`, plus `currencies.json` and `site.json`.
- `content/world-starred.json` lists every Michelin-starred restaurant in the world: name, stars, position and Michelin link. It drives the outlined "no prices yet" pins on the homepage map. It also gives the homepage's country rows for countries without a page yet (star counts and a button that frames them on the map); `WORLD_COUNTRIES` in build.py maps the guide's country names to our ids and English/Chinese names, and the build lists any new country it doesn't know. Below the rows, folded under one heading, `content/no-stars.json` lists the UN member countries with no starred restaurant, by region, with an optional `note`/`noteZh` on why (most simply have no MICHELIN Guide; checked 4 Oct 2026 from the guide's own search, where only Saudi Arabia has a guide without stars), and an optional `guide` (a guide's id) that links the country's name to it. Starless countries with search demand get an explainer guide rather than a place page, which would add an empty country card: `michelin-star-restaurants-india`, `-australia` and `-south-africa` (5 Oct 2026, plus `michelin-star-indian-restaurants-london`). Brief 15 (9 Oct 2026, to-do item guide-no-stars-explainers) added `michelin-star-restaurants-seattle` (no guide), `-phoenix` (Arizona is in the Southwest guide but has no stars), `-indianapolis` (Great Lakes guide, first stars 21 April 2027), `-indonesia` (Bali and Jakarta), `-saudi-arabia` (a guide but no stars), `-ottawa-calgary-halifax` (Canada beyond its three guides) and the round-up `countries-without-michelin-stars` (Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Nigeria, Kenya, Egypt, Romania), whose countries link to their sections from no-stars.json with `guide: "id#section"`. Their nearest-stars lines use `{{in_<place>}}` and `{{from_<place>}}`. Recheck each after its guide's next ceremony (Saudi Arabia and Great Lakes are in ceremonies.json). When one gains stars, add its place pages, rewrite the guide as launch coverage linking to them, and drop it from no-stars.json. The build hides any country that has gained stars and asks for it to be deleted from that file. The rows are grouped by continent (`CONTINENTS` in build.py; the build lists any country missing from it) and can be sorted A–Z, by starred restaurants, or by three-, two- or one-star restaurants, high to low or (clicking the chosen button again) low to high; the counts used come from the whole guide (`guide` on each country, from world-starred.json), since our pages may cover only some cities. Our own restaurants are matched by name and position (`same_restaurant` in build.py) and shown as filled pins with prices instead. To refresh it after big guide releases, follow `scripts/michelin_world.js` and `scripts/receive.py`. guide.michelin.com blocks curl, so the extraction runs in a browser tab and is posted to a local receiver.
- `src/` holds the HTML templates (`place.html`, `home.html`) and the shared `assets/`:
  - `common.js` has the settings, all English/Chinese wording (`I18N`) and helpers.
  - `place.js` runs destination pages and `home.js` runs the homepage.
  - The homepage (7 Oct 2026, made shorter on phones: 18,000px to about 5,000px): hero and search; "ways in" tiles for Near me, Help me pick, Compare and Guides, then "Popular destinations" links (`HOME_POPULAR`, the most-searched places), from `home_ways_html()`; the world map; every country as one short row (name, cheapest dinner menu, star counts, a button that unfolds its regions and cities), folded by continent (`home_countries_html()`; open on computers, folded on phones; `#dest-<id>` opens its continent); "Starred for less", the cheapest set menu at each star level and the biggest three-star lunch saving, worked out at build time (`home_less_html()`); six guide cards (`HOME_GUIDES`, `home_guides_html()`, swiped sideways on phones); then the wishlist, which shrinks to a line or two when empty. All of it is written into the page by build.py so Google sees the links; home.js only re-sorts the country rows (`sortDestRows()`). Clicks on these are counted as `home-link` events.
  - `site.css` holds all styles.
  - The header: on computers the top row and the breadcrumb bar (`.switchbar`: breadcrumbs, currency, language, Install app) both stay on screen, inside `.masthead`. On phones and tablets (1140px and below, where the full header no longer fits one line) only one row stays: logo, search (jumps to the page's search box, else the homepage's `#search`), wishlist and a menu button (`#menuBtn`) that drops down the section links, Sign in and Share (`.header-menu`; on tablets a panel at the right). The breadcrumb bar scrolls away. On phones (720px and below) the wishlist shows just its heart, and the currency switch moves to a small picker beside Dinner/Lunch (`#currencyPick`). Keep all six page templates' headers the same shape.
- `build.py` uses only the standard library and must stay **Python 3.9 compatible**, because that's the Mac's version. It checks the data, then writes `_site/`: one page per place (places with no stars yet get a short "none yet" page), the homepage, the sitemap (each address with its last-changed day, worked out from the git history by `sitemap_dates()`, which is why Cloudflare's build command starts with `git fetch --unshallow`) and the 404 page. Bad data stops the build with a plain-English list of problems.
- Guides (`content/guides/<id>.json`, rendered by `build_guides()` with `src/guide.html`): explainer articles at `/guides/<id>/`, plus a list at `/guides/`, English only, with Article, FAQPage and breadcrumb JSON-LD. Their figures (`{{n3}}`, `{{price1}}`…) are filled from the restaurant data by `guide_stats()` (in the title, h1, description and summary too, so the three-star list's title keeps its count current; the build counts a figure as four characters of the 60), and `<a data-guide="id">` links switch on once that guide exists (README lists both). Write them to the owner's content briefs (Google Doc "Michelin Star Site: First-Batch Content Briefs": house style, then one brief per page): answer first in 40–50 words, stars only from the MICHELIN Guide, prices with US dollars, every table dated. Every page's footer links to the "What is a Michelin star?" pillar. English destination pages end with a "Related guides" box (`related_guides()` in build.py, `.related-guides` in site.css): guides whose `places` include the page or a place above it, then guides whose published article (tables included, `guide_bodies()`) links to the page, the world three-star list where there's a three-star restaurant and the by-country guide, up to 6. Each guide lists its brief's target `keywords` (main one first); the build prints any that don't appear in the page. The list guides (by country, three-star world/UK/London) drop in tables with `{{table:name}}`, built from the restaurant data and matched to MICHELIN Guide links via world-starred.json. In those tables each restaurant's name links to its row on our destination page (`restaurant_link()`: `/uk/england/london/#r=<id>`, which place.js opens, scrolls to and highlights), with a small ↗ beside it to its MICHELIN Guide page. The Green Star is being phased out (Michelin, 18 May 2026, replaced by the Mindful Voices editorial platform), so write about it in the past tense. Brief 7's city guides are covered by the destination pages, not separate guides. Each guide has a featured picture (`guide_image()`): an AI illustration in the house style (`docs/image-style.md`, made in Canva, design "Starred Bill guide images"), sized by `scripts/guide_images.py` into `src/img/guides/<id>.jpg`, `-card.jpg` and `-og.jpg` (the full-size `-src.jpg` originals are gitignored), with `imageAlt` in the guide's JSON.

## Data rules
- A restaurant file's name is its id, and visitors' wishlists store it. Never rename a published restaurant file.
- Prices are numbers in the country's currency, per person, before service. Record where each price came from:
  - `sourceType`: `site` (the restaurant's own website), `press` (a review or booking site) or `none`.
  - `source`: the link.
  - The lunch equivalents are `lunchSourceType` and `lunchSource`.
- `noPairing: true` says a restaurant doesn't offer a wine pairing: receipts, the wishlist bill, Compare and restaurant pages then say "no pairing offered" (`rcptNoPairingOffered`) instead of "no pairing listed". Set it only on positive evidence (no alcohol served, or à la carte, street food or a casual local place with no set menu to pair with): a website that never mentions a pairing isn't enough, as spot checks on 9 Oct 2026 found pairings at most such places. That day's pass (starred-bill-research/wine-pairing: crawl.py, classify.py, review.py, decisions.txt, no_pairing.txt, apply.py) added 66 pairing prices from the restaurants' own sites, marked 65 restaurants, fixed 17 menu prices that held a pairing or menu-with-wine price, and lists in worth-looking-up.txt the restaurants whose sites mention a pairing without a price.
- When a restaurant loses its stars or closes, keep it: set `stars` to 0 and fill in `status`, `formerStars` and `statusNote`. Ones still open (`lost` or `changed`) that lost their stars in the last three years (by `changeDate`) get small grey pins on the homepage and destination maps (`lostPin` in common.js), so give them `lat`/`lng`.
- A restaurant's `city` is a place id. It shows on that place and on every place above it, plus any `group` that includes them. A region or country can hold restaurants directly: Hong Kong, Macau, Singapore, Ireland, and UK restaurants outside the cities we cover. These sit in their county where it has a page (e.g. `yorkshire`, `cumbria`, `perthshire`), otherwise in their nation (`england`, `scotland`, `wales`, `northern-ireland` or `channel-islands`), with "Town, County" in `area`. ## Accounts
Free accounts let people keep their wishlist on every device and tick off restaurants they've been to ("been there").
- Supabase project `uvdaclbhskkukyngikhq` (London) holds sign-in and one table, `saved`: one row per person per restaurant (`wishlist`, `visited`, `visited_on`), keyed on the restaurant's file name. Its row-level security lets each person read and change only their own rows. `supabase/schema.sql` creates it (run in Supabase's SQL Editor); `delete_my_account()` lets someone delete themselves.
- `src/assets/account.js` loads the Supabase library from jsDelivr only when someone is signed in or signing in, signs in by an emailed 6-digit code (typed into the sign-in box, so it works inside the home-screen app, whose storage is separate from the browser's) or the email's link, or Google, and keeps the browser copies (`starredbill-wishlist`, `starredbill-visited` in localStorage) in step with the account. Pages keep reading the browser copies through `loadWishlist()` / `loadVisited()` and save through `setWishlist()` / `setVisited()` in common.js, which fire `sb:wishlist` / `sb:visited` events; `sb:account` fires when someone signs in or out.
- The first time a browser meets an account, anything saved while signed out is merged in; after that the account is the record. Changes wait in `starredbill-pending` until sent. Signing out clears both lists from that browser.
- Signed out, the wishlist works and stays in the browser; "been there" asks the person to sign up.
- The free Supabase plan pauses after about a week without activity, so `.github/workflows/supabase-keepalive.yml` asks the database one question every three days (it can also be run by hand from the Actions tab).
- `/account/` (account.html + account-page.js) shows stats, milestones, progress by destination, both lists, and download / sign out / delete. `/privacy/` (privacy.html + info-page.js) is the privacy notice in English and Chinese; update it if what's stored changes.
- In Supabase, Authentication › URL Configuration allows starredbill.com, www and localhost:8799. Google sign-in uses a Google Cloud OAuth client; sign-in emails go through Resend's SMTP from starredbill.com (set up 3 Oct 2026). Both sign-in emails (Magic Link and Confirm signup, under Authentication › Emails › Templates) use `supabase/email-template.html`, which shows the code (`{{ .Token }}`) above the link.
